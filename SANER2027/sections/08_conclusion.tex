\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied retrieval-augmented few-shot prompting (\rag) as a training-free alternative to LoRA fine-tuning for issue report classification (\irc). We conducted an empirical study with four Qwen2.5-Instruct models of 3B to 32B parameters and 6{,}600 issue reports from eleven projects, taking the labeled issues either from the target project alone or from all eleven projects. Zero-shot prompting and a similarity-weighted vote over the neighbors' labels (\knn) served as reference points for what the LLM and retrieval each contribute. Because \rag, like prior methods, most often labels questions as bugs, we also evaluated a variant (\frag) that removes bug-labeled examples when the neighbors suggest a question.

With this filter, retrieval-augmented few-shot prompting matches the macro~$F_1$ of LoRA fine-tuning while using $11\times$ less labeled data per project, no training, and 33\% less peak GPU memory on average. Using only the target project's labeled issues, \frag\ trails fine-tuning on the labeled issues of all eleven projects by 1.0 point, a difference that is not statistically significant, while \rag\ trails by 2.8 points. Per size, fine-tuning is significantly ahead at Qwen-7B and Qwen-14B, and \frag\ is ahead at the other two sizes, but not significantly. Given the same labeled issues, both RAG variants outperform fine-tuning at every model size. Two earlier results lead to this one. Retrieved examples improve the LLM over both reference points at every number of examples, mostly on questions, and the filter further reduces question-to-bug errors at the cost of bug recall.

The two approaches make different errors. Fine-tuning finds more bugs but over-predicts the bug label, whereas \frag\ makes fewer false bug predictions at most model sizes, and the question-to-bug confusion persists under every method we test. \rag's inference is slower, and at Qwen-32B its total time exceeds fine-tuning's even when training is included, while fine-tuning must be repeated whenever labels are added or the model is replaced. Future work can test these findings on other LLM families and quantization settings, on imbalanced, minority-class, and multi-label data, and on other issue trackers such as Jira and Bugzilla. Further directions include combining retrieval with a fine-tuned model, re-ranking the neighbors with an LLM, other embedding models and similarity metrics, and other prompt structures such as chain-of-thought prompting~\cite{koyuncu2025exploring}.
